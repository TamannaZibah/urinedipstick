Urinalysis Fundamentals for Medical Assistants

Urinalysis (UA) is a routine laboratory test performed in outpatient clinics and hospitals to screen for urinary tract infections (UTIs), kidney disease, and diabetes. As a Medical Assistant, you will conduct the Physical Examination and the Chemical 10-Parameter Dipstick Examination.

The 1-Hour Rule

Test urine within 1 hour of collection. If delayed, refrigerate at 2°C to 8°C (36°F–46°F) for up to 4 hours.

Warm to Room Temp

Refrigerated urine must return to room temperature (15–30 min) before dipping. Cold urine slows reagent pad chemical reactions.

Required PPE

Always wear clean gloves, lab coat/gown, and safety goggles or face shield whenever handling unpreserved body fluids.

Standard Office Urinalysis Supplies

  • Multistix 10SG Reagent Strips: Kept in original bottle with desiccant cap tightly closed to block moisture.
  • Clean Absorbent Paper Towel: For tapping the side edge of the dipped strip to remove excess runoff urine.
  • Timer or Watch with Second Hand: Crucial for recording pads at exact 30, 40, 45, 60, and 120-second marks.
  • Color Reference Comparator Chart: Stamped on the bottle or automated analyzer (Clinitek).
  • Biohazard Waste Container: For safe disposal of used test strips, cups, and gloves.

Daily Quality Control (QC) Rules

To ensure patient safety under CLIA-waived regulations, the Medical Assistant must verify test strip accuracy using manufactured control solutions:

1. Level 1 (Negative Control): Must yield all negative/normal results.

2. Level 2 (Positive Control): Must produce expected positive color reactions.

When to run QC: At the start of each testing day, whenever opening a new bottle lot number, and if results appear questionable.

Never report patient results if QC results fail or are expired!

The 10 Reagent Parameters & Standard Read Times

Pads must be read at specified time intervals. Reading prematurely or past the time limit causes false positives or false negatives.

Chemical Urinalysis Reagents, Read Times, and Clinical Meanings
Standard Read Time Parameter Normal Finding Clinical Significance / When Elevated
30 seconds Glucose (GLU) Negative Diabetes Mellitus (renal threshold exceeded >180 mg/dL).
30 seconds Bilirubin (BIL) Negative Liver damage, hepatitis, or biliary duct obstruction.
40 seconds Ketones (KET) Negative Fat metabolism: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA), starvation, vomiting.
45 seconds Specific Gravity (SG) 1.005 – 1.030 Kidney concentration: <1.005 (overhydrated), >1.030 (dehydrated).
60 seconds Blood / Hemoglobin (BLO) Negative Kidney stones, infection, trauma, menses, or glomerulonephritis.
60 seconds pH 4.5 – 8.0 (avg 6.0) Acid-base status. Alkaline urine may indicate old sample or Proteus UTI.
60 seconds Protein / Albumin (PRO) Negative or Trace Early marker for renal disease, preeclampsia in pregnancy, or fever.
60 seconds Urobilinogen (URO) 0.2 – 1.0 mg/dL Elevated in liver disease or hemolytic anemias.
60 seconds Nitrite (NIT) Negative Gram-negative bacteria (e.g., E. coli) converting nitrate to nitrite (UTI).
120 seconds (2 min) Leukocytes (LEU) Negative Presence of white blood cells (pyuria) indicating urinary tract infection.

Urine Drug Screen (UDS) & Custody

Forensic Standard
The 4-Minute Temperature Window: Check the temperature strip on the side of the collection cup within 4 minutes of donation. Acceptable range: 90°F to 100°F (32°C to 38°C). Outside this range indicates potential dilution, substitution, or tampering.

Chain-of-Custody (CoC) Protocol:

• Verify donor photo ID and have donor empty all pockets and remove bulky outerwear.

• Add bluing agent to toilet bowl and secure sink water faucets.

• Collect at least 30–45 mL of urine.

• Apply tamper-evident security seal over lid in donor's presence; both donor and MA sign and date form.
